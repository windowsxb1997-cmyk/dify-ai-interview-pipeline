Mock Interview Transcript — Persona 1: Immersive Training Type
Interviewee: Derek M. (ID: US-SEA-011), 27, software engineer, Seattle
I = Interviewer, R = Respondent

I: When was the last time you wore over-ear headphones at the gym?

R: This morning. I lift six times a week at a commercial gym near my office — an LA Fitness. I never train without my Sony WH-1000XM5. Never. The gym is loud, the music is terrible, people drop plates. With ANC on and my playlist, it's just me and the bar. Total immersion.

I: Walk me through a session.

R: I get there at 6 AM. Headphones on before I even swipe in. Warm-up, then compounds — squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press. Three hours sometimes on weekends. The ANC is non-negotiable. If I forgot to charge them, I'd honestly rather skip the gym. Music tempo drives my intensity — heavy hip-hop for heavy sets.

I: Any annoyances?

R: Bench press. When I lie back, the band slides toward my forehead. I've adapted — I push it forward before unracking. And the ear pads get sweaty after two hours; I wipe them down with a towel between sessions and use alcohol wipes weekly. Heat is a minor thing, the gym has good AC.

I: Rank your priorities: noise cancelling, sound quality, stability, weight, clamping comfort, heat dissipation, sweat resistance, cleaning, style, battery, price, brand.

R: ANC first, by a mile. Sound quality second — bass matters for intensity. Stability third, only because of bench. Everything else is noise. Pun intended.

I: Would you buy a headphone designed for the gym?

R: If something had better clamp stability for bench and same ANC, I'd pay real money — maybe $400. My XM5 is close to perfect for lifting. I use it only for the gym and home; commuting I use earbuds, the Sony is too bulky for the bus.

I: Friends who also wear over-ears at the gym?

R: Two of my lifting buddies do. Same reason — immersion. Once you train with ANC, you can't go back.

[End]
